AEGIS Percentile Compliance Audit — Oxford Guardians Platform

Section-by-section completeness scoring · 27 July 2026 · supersedes the audits of 22 and 25 June 2026

METHOD Every numbered requirement of the 76-page AEGIS Accreditation Handbook & Guardianship Organisation Quality Standards (September 2025) was decomposed into the specific fields, records and functionalities the handbook actually names — including the itemised lists in Standards 5.2.1, 5.5.1, 5.10.2, 6.8.1 and Appendices 5, 7, 11 and 13. Each requirement is scored as (required elements present ÷ required elements named). A requirement whose page or store does not exist scores 0%. A field that exists but is unstructured, unwired or unpersisted scores partial credit. Sub-section scores are the unweighted mean of their requirements; the standard score is the unweighted mean of its sub-sections; the headline score is the unweighted mean of all 43 scored sections.
SCOPE NOTE — WHERE THE PLATFORM ACTUALLY LIVES Despite the directory name, the working application is old_version/ — index.html at the repository root is only the sign-in shell and redirects to old_version/admin-dashboard.html. All scoring below is against old_version/ and old_version/assets/db.js. The copy at assets/db.js (root) is an orphan: no page loads it, and it has diverged — it still carries seeded contract records that the live store does not.

WHAT SCORES WELL Four areas are genuinely close to inspection-ready: Homestay annual training (5.9 — 90%), Homestay visits & inspections (5.11 — 85%), Finance (2.9 — 83%) and Homestay H&S / annual visit (5.10 — 72%). The H&S checklist covers all twelve of the checks named at 5.10.2.1–5.10.2.12 with no gaps, and the annual-visit report writes back to the host record so annualVisitLast/Due/Status stay accurate. Private fostering (Std 8 — 69%) and Parent liaison (Std 10 — 67%) follow.
THE THREE FINDINGS THAT MOVE THE SCORE MOST 1. Contracts cannot be created (Std 2.2 — 25%). The contracts module has a schema, a dashboard, per-profile tabs, a version pill, a status lifecycle and a genuinely good safeguarding block in saveContract() — but the "New contract" control is href="#", the store seeds empty (og_contracts_seed_version = 'v2-empty'), and no page in the platform ever assigns entityType. Nothing can enter the store, so all four contract requirements are unmeetable.
2. No concern can be logged (Std 6.5 — 19%). saveConcern() and generateConcernId() exist in db.js but are called by no page. low-level-concern-create.html has no db.js. The concerns dashboards are read-only over four seeded records, and the "Escalate to low-level concern" outcome on welfare-note-create.html does not write one. This is a regression against the 22 June audit, which scored 6.5.2 as functional.
3. The SCR reports two columns as permanently blank (Appendix 7 — 42%). On scr-hosts, scr-staff and scr-drivers the DOB and Self-declaration cells are hardcoded — (lines 192 and 202) even though selfDeclarationDocPath and selfDeclarationDate are captured on host-compliance-declaration.html. The SCR under-reports evidence the platform already holds.

What The Scores Show

The distribution is bimodal, and the split is not random

Sections score high when the requirement is an event with a form — a visit, a training session, a check-in, a payment, a notification. Every section above 60% is of that shape: annual training (90%), inspection co-operation (85%), finance (83%), H&S and annual visit (72%), student wellbeing (75%), private fostering (69%), parent liaison (67%), safeguarding training (68%). The platform is built by someone who models events well.

Sections score near zero when the requirement is a governing document or a standing condition of the household. Every section below 20% is one of those two: handbooks (3%), student behaviour (0%), anti-bullying (0%), complaints (3.3%), whistleblowing (5%), Prevent (7.5%), laundry (0%), conduct when hosting (4.2%), meals (17.5%), accommodation conditions (15.8%). There is no policy or document store of any kind in db.js — no company-level entity exists at all — and the host schema models compliance evidence but not living conditions.

Those two absences explain roughly two thirds of the missing score. Neither is a hard engineering problem.

Three items are near-complete work that has been left unwired

These cost the score more than they cost to fix, because the hard part is already written:

  1. Contracts (2.2, 25%) — a full schema, dashboard, lifecycle, versioning and a working safeguarding block in saveContract(), reachable by nothing. One creation form turns four requirements from 25% to ~85% and closes four Appendix A submission items.
  2. Concerns (6.5, 18.8%) — saveConcern() and generateConcernId() are written and exported but called by no page, while welfare-note-create.html already collects the escalation decision. Wiring that one outcome into saveConcern() restores the safeguarding register and reverses the regression against the June audit.
  3. SCR blanks (Appendix 7, 42%) — scr-hosts, scr-staff and scr-drivers hardcode — in the DOB and Self-declaration cells (lines 192 and 202), so the SCR under-reports a self-declaration the platform has already captured and alerts on. The self-declaration cell is a one-line fix; DOB needs a schema field.

Two structural defects cap the achievable score

  1. Storage and access control. Every record lives in browser localStorage — unencrypted, single-browser, no authentication, no roles, no backup, lost on cache clear. audit-log.html, the page that would evidence an audit trail, has no db.js and is itself a mockup. This alone caps 2.5.1, 2.7.4, 3.4.3 and 6.2.6, and it is why safeguarding case notes naming students and disclosures are readable by anyone who opens the browser. No amount of feature work raises those four while it stands.
  2. No company-level entity. db.js models students, staff, hosts, drivers, parents, agents, schools and their events — but nothing models Oxford Guardians itself. That is why insurance (2.4.1), ICO registration (2.7.2), the data protection policy (2.7.3), policy review (2.10.1), significant changes (2.11.1) and every handbook and policy in Standard 6 all score at or near zero: there is nowhere to put them. A single og_org_documents store with title, category, publication date, review date, version and file would lift a dozen sections at once and produce most of the Appendix A submission set.

READING THIS SCORE HONESTLY 33.7% measures how much of what AEGIS names is represented in the platform. It is not a prediction of inspection outcome, and it is not a judgement on build quality — the homestay H&S, annual-visit, training and private-fostering modules are better than most production compliance software. But an inspector does not average. They ask for the Appendix A submission set, and the platform currently holds none of its thirty documents; they ask to see the complaints record, and there is no complaints store; they ask how safeguarding case notes are restricted to the DSL, and every record is in unprotected browser storage. On the requirements that decide accreditation rather than the ones that fill a dashboard, the platform is further behind than 33.7% suggests.
